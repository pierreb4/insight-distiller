In any comparison, the familiar option arrives **pre-steelmanned** — the model (or person)
knows it cold and states it at its best — while a novel option arrives **half-baked**, in
its first rough form. A judge scoring "confidence" or absolute quality then penalizes the
**articulation gap**, not the idea's merit. The comparison is rigged toward the familiar
*before* merit enters — a large part of why LLMs (and teams) silently drop long shots, on
top of the raw mode-seeking bias.

The fix: **equalize articulation effort before judging.** Give each long shot an
**advocate** that develops it to its strongest defensible form — tighten the mechanism,
name the precise conditions under which it beats the standard approach, sharpen its cheapest
falsifying test — *before* the comparative judge sees it. Only then is the contest about
merit rather than rehearsal.

This generalizes well beyond idea generation: incumbent-vs-challenger technology choices,
reviewing a familiar vs an unfamiliar design, or weighing your own prior approach against a
new one (status-quo bias is the same articulation asymmetry plus sunk cost). Anywhere one
option is better-rehearsed than another, the rehearsal — not the merit — is doing the work.

Pairs with comparative (not in-isolation) judging — rank a set against itself rather than
scoring each 1–5 in a vacuum, which also favors the safe option
([[generate-a-comparison-set-select-ruthlessly]]). It is the evaluation-side companion to
[[engineer-exploration-over-exploitation]], which handles the generation side. Loosely a
[[suspect-the-measurement-before-the-artifact]] case where the "measurement" is the
comparison itself, biased by articulation effort.
